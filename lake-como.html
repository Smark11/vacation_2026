A family guide · Lake Como · July 5–9, 2026

Lake Como

The most beautiful of the Italian lakes — steep green mountains plunging into deep blue water, grand villa gardens, and ferries criss-crossing all day. Cool, scenic and entirely car-free, with no thrill ride in sight: just boats, gardens, swimming and a funicular into the hills.

4 nights · Jul 5–92 adults + 1 nine-year-old · no car needed

Know before you go

The scenic one

Lake Como is the upside-down-Y of deep blue water where the Alps meet the Lombardy plain — a place of wooded cliffs, pastel villages stacked at the waterline, and the great lakeside villas of two centuries of dukes, divas and (lately) film crews. Against Garda it trades theme-park thrills and warm shallow beaches for sheer beauty, cooler air, and the best car-free ferry network in Italy. For a kid who'd rather ride a boat than a roller-coaster, it's a gentle, gorgeous fit — and it's the closest of the lakes to your Milan flight.

📅 When & how long

Peak July — sunny, busy, but tempered by lake breeze and mountain air. Four nights is plenty: a base in the "centro lago," days spent ferry-hopping villas and towns, plus a boat day and a swim.

Getting there & back · car-free

Coast to the lake

One easy spine: down the coast to Milan, then north up the Valtellina line to Varenna, right on the water. About four and a half hours, one or two changes — no car, no fuss.

The routing: Monterosso down to Milano Centrale (~3 h, typically one change along the coast), then the Milan–Sondrio/Tirano regional (roughly hourly) to Varenna-Esino — a pretty run that hugs the east shore for the last stretch. Why Varenna: it's the only "centro lago" village with its own train station, so you arrive in the heart of the beautiful middle lake without a transfer by bus or boat. (Como town also has stations — Como S. Giovanni / Como Nord Lago — but it's the busy city end, an hour's ferry from the prettiest reach.)

Getting around · the ferry lake

Ferries & where to base

Como is made for car-free travel — the whole "centro lago" runs on ferries, all day, every day. The only real decision is which town you sleep in. Here's how the network works, and our honest pick.

The hub
The centro-lago triangle

Varenna ↔ Bellagio ↔ Menaggio sit a short hop apart where the lake's three arms meet. Frequent passenger ferries (Varenna–Bellagio ~15–20 min) plus a car-ferry connect them — the heart of any Como stay.

Ferries
Navigazione Laghi

The state operator runs slow boats, fast hydrofoils & car-ferries. A central-lake day pass (~€15) lets you hop Varenna/Bellagio/Menaggio/Cadenabbia all day; buy at the pier. Last boats are early evening — check the timetable before a late dinner across the water.

Train vs ferry
Who's on the rails

Only Varenna, Como & Lecco have train stations. Bellagio, Menaggio, Lenno & Tremezzo are ferry- or bus-only — lovely to stay in, but you arrive (with bags) by boat.

The verdict

Base in Varenna, ferry to everything

It's the one centro-lago town you reach (and leave) by train, it's walkable and scenic, and it's the gateway to the Varenna–Bellagio–Menaggio ferry triangle — so every villa, garden and town is a short boat ride away. It's also calmer and better value than Bellagio. If a pool matters more than a walkable old town, look at Menaggio/Tremezzina (west-shore value, lido swimming, the villas next door) or a Como-town pool hotel; if money's no object and the icon is the point, Bellagio.

The captain's day

Your own wooden lake

Como's the lake of the classic varnished motorboat — and with Dad's licence, you don't need a tour. Take the helm of your own little boat and nose into coves the ferries never reach.

How it works

Same national rule as Garda: a rental boat up to ~40 hp (30 kW) needs no licence — just ID, a card deposit and a short safety briefing — which sidesteps any question about a US licence. Verified self-drive operators include Boat Hire Bellagio and the Water Experience Center (and a Varenna-pickup option); reckon roughly €280 for a 4-hour half-day (~€520 full day) plus fuel, on a boat that seats well over a family of three (confirm 2026 rates and ask for a child life jacket).

Idle past the Villa del Balbianello on its wooded point (you'll recognise it from the films), drop into a quiet swimming cove off the Comacina island, and wave at the grand hotels from the water. If you'd rather not drive, the classic wooden taxi-boat tours from Bellagio or Varenna do the same circuit with a skipper.

Timing
Mind the Breva

Como's afternoon south wind, the Breva, freshens the lower lake from midday; the morning Tivano blows gently north. Calmest water and best light are early — launch in the morning.

The run
The Balbianello reach

From Lenno/Sala Comacina the loveliest water is right there — the Balbianello headland, the Comacina island, the Tremezzina villa shore. Short, sheltered, scenic.

Safety
Busy water

Como gets ferry and speedboat traffic — keep clear of the ferry lanes and piers, slow near swimmers, life jackets on the crew, and keep an eye up-lake for the Breva line.

🎬
For the small film buff

That villa on the wooded point — Villa del Balbianello — is a movie star. It's the lakeside hospital where James Bond recovers in Casino Royale, and the secret house where Anakin and Padmé get married in Star Wars: Attack of the Clones. From your own boat you can float right past the terrace where they filmed, on the most beautiful corner of the most beautiful lake in Italy. Not a bad thing to tell the class in September.

The best of the lake

What to see & do

No theme park, and none needed — Como's draws are gardens you wander, a funicular you ride, water you swim in, and boats everywhere. Gentle, scenic, and good for a nine-year-old who likes a boat over a coaster.

The icon

Villa del Balbianello

The most beautiful villa on the lake, on a wooded promontory at Lenno — terraced gardens, a loggia with a view down both arms, and a film-set pedigree (Star Wars, Casino Royale). Reach it by a short walk or a wooden taxi-boat from Lenno.

Plan itGardens (and the optional villa interior) are timed — book ahead; closed some days. The boat approach is half the magic.
Gardens

Villa Carlotta & Villa Melzi

Two great garden villas a ferry-hop apart: Carlotta at Tremezzo — terraced botanical gardens, a fountain, citrus and a kid-pleasing grotto — and Melzi at Bellagio, a romantic lakeside park of statues and giant trees. Room to run, shade to flop in.

TipBoth are right by their ferry piers — easy to chain in one relaxed day.
Up high

The Como–Brunate funicular

From Como town, a steep little funicular railway (since 1894) climbs to the village of Brunate for a hawk's-eye view over the whole lake. A short, fun ride up, a gelato and a viewpoint at the top, an easy stroll to the lighthouse — a proper kid highlight with no white knuckles.

Pair itWith a Como-town day: the cathedral, the silk museum, and Tempio Voltiano.
Swim & stroll

Lidos & lakeside walks

Cool off at a lido — Menaggio's pool-and-grass lakefront, Bellagio's little beach, or Villa Olmo's in Como. Then walk Varenna's stilted "Lovers' Walk," or a stretch of the Greenway del Lago, the flat shoreline path through the Tremezzina villages.

NoteComo's water is cool and clean; lidos give you grass, shade, ladders and a snack bar — easiest with a kid.
More, if you've the time
  • Greenway del Lago di Como — an easy ~10 km walking path (Colonno→Griante) through old villages and villa gates; do a short, flat stretch with the kid, picnic, and ferry back.
  • Madonna del Ghisallo (above Bellagio) — the tiny chapel that's the patron shrine of cyclists, with a quirky bike museum and a huge view; a bus-and-walk for a clear morning.
  • Como silk museum & Tempio Voltiano — Como invented modern silk; the museum shows how, and the Volta temple celebrates the local who gave us the battery (the "volt" is named for him). Two quietly brilliant rainy-day stops.
Together · hands-on with a 9-year-old

Things to do as a crew

Not sights you look at — things you do, together. Como is full of gentle, hands-on, no-thrill ways for the three of you to spend a morning or an afternoon as a team.

🍝

Make pasta together · a cooking class

The family highlight in the kitchen: a hands-on pasta class where all three of you roll, fill and cook your own fresh pasta (plus a daily dessert) — then sit down and eat what you made. Classes around Bellagio, Tremezzina/Mezzegra and Cernobbio are explicitly kid-welcoming (little ones get their own dough to play with), often in a family home or a school with lake views.

Book it · ~½ day, from ~€140–160 pp · lunch or dinner sitting · reserve ahead

Earn the down-time at a lakeside lido — Menaggio's has a pool, grass, ladders into the lake and a snack bar; Bellagio and Como (Villa Olmo) have their own. The easiest way to actually get in the water with a kid.

🗺️
A game for the week

Give the 9-year-old a notebook and two jobs. One: draw Lake Como's upside-down Y and mark, each day, which town you slept in, which villas you saw, and the route your boat took. Two: become the family's ferry captain — at every pier, they read the timetable board, pick which boat you catch, and tell you when to be back for the last one. By Thursday they'll know the lake better than you do.

A suggested rhythm

Your four days

Base in Varenna and let the ferries set the pace — a villa day, a boat day, a funicular day, with swims and gelato in the gaps. Mornings on the water before the day-trippers and the Breva arrive; long golden evenings once they leave.

  • Sun · Jul 5 · arrive
    In from Monterosso (~4½ h), settle into Varenna
    Train via Milan to Varenna-Esino, walk down to the harbour. Drop the bags, stroll the Lovers' Walk, first gelato by the water, dinner watching the ferries cross.
  • Mon · Jul 6 · villas by ferry
    Villa Carlotta + Bellagio & Villa Melzi
    A classic centro-lago day: ferry to Tremezzo for Villa Carlotta's gardens, across to Bellagio for the stair-streets and Villa Melzi, gelato on the point, ferry home. Pure scenery, zero rides.
  • Tue · Jul 7 · the boat day
    Captain your own boat to the Balbianello reach
    Rent a small motorboat (no licence under 40 hp), launch in the calm morning, float past Villa del Balbianello and the Comacina island, and swim off the boat in a quiet cove. Back before the Breva builds.
  • Wed · Jul 8 · the city & the heights
    Como town + the Brunate funicular
    Ferry or train down to Como: the funicular up to Brunate for the big view, the cathedral, the silk museum or Tempio Voltiano, a lido swim. Or swap in a Greenway walk and a lazy lido day if the kid's had enough sightseeing.
  • Thu · Jul 9 · westward
    A last lake morning, then on to Milan
    One more ferry or a swim, then the easy run to Milan (~1 h to Centrale) and on to your final-night base near the airport — Como's closeness to Malpensa makes this the easiest lake finish of all.
⛴️
For the small navigator

Lake Como is shaped like an upside-down letter Y — two long legs of water and a pointed bit in the middle where the town of Bellagio sits. It's one of the deepest lakes in all of Europe: more than 400 metres down in places — deep enough to sink the Eiffel Tower and still have water over the top. All week you'll cross from one arm to another on the ferries; see if you can draw the Y from memory by the time you leave, and mark where you went each day.

À table · the lake kitchen

Lake fish & risotto

Como cooks what it catches and grows on the slopes — freshwater fish, saffrony risottos, polenta, and gelato by the water. Gentle, savoury, kid-friendly.

Risotto con pesce persico
The lake's signature: creamy butter-and-Parmigiano risotto topped with little pan-fried fillets of perch. Mild and buttery — a reliable kid win.
Lavarello & missoltini
Lavarello (whitefish), simply grilled, is the gentle kid option; missoltini — sun-dried, salted agone grilled with polenta — is the very local, grown-up one.
Polenta & toc
Mountain comfort food: polenta uncia with butter and cheese, and toc, a soft cheesy polenta stirred at the table. Warming on a breezy lake evening.
Wood-fired pizza
Every lake town has a good wood-oven pizzeria — the failsafe family dinner when the 9-year-old has had enough fish. A Margherita by the water never misses.
Fresh pasta & pizzoccheri
Hand-made pastas everywhere; up-valley, look for pizzoccheri — buckwheat ribbons with cabbage, potato and mountain cheese. Hearty Valtellina cooking.
Gelato & the aperitivo
Gelato on every quay, and the lakeside aperitivo at sunset — a spritz for the grown-ups, a view for everyone. Como's evening ritual.

A moment to seek

The last ferry of the evening, riding home to Varenna. The day-trippers are gone, the lake has turned to pewter and rose, and the mountains on the far shore are going blue-black against the sky. Your daughter is up at the rail in the warm wind, picking out the villages by their lights and asking which arm of the Y you're on, and somewhere behind a headland the Balbianello you sailed past this morning is lighting its lamps. The engine thrums, a church bell carries across the water, and the whole impossible blue postcard of it slides by at walking pace, just for you. Tomorrow there are gardens; tonight there is only this — the most beautiful lake in Italy, and a kid who got to drive a boat across it.
