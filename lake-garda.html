Why the lake, and why the south

Italy's inland sea

Leave the salt of the Ligurian coast behind and head north to a long blue funnel cupped between vineyards at the bottom and Dolomite cliffs at the top — fifty-two kilometres of water so big the Romans called it a sea. After two weeks of trains and stairs and packing, this is the half of the trip where you finally get the keys: a single base, shallow warm beaches, a theme park next door, walled towns a boat-ride apart, gelato on the ramparts, and absolutely nowhere you have to be. You're parking yourselves at the warm, gentle southern tip, where the lake is widest and calmest and everything a family wants is within a flat walk, a short ferry, or your own rented hull.

The shape of the water

Garda is shaped like a funnel because that's how the last Ice Age left it — a glacier ground a deep trough through the Alps, and the moraine hills bottled up the south end, leaving the lake wide and shallow and sun-warmed at the bottom, narrow and fjord-deep at the top. The Romans summered here: the poet Catullus kept a villa on the Sirmione headland in the 1st century BC, and the ruins of a grand Roman house still spread through an olive grove at the point. The medieval Scaligeri lords of Verona studded the shore with swallow-tailed castles; the Venetians fortified Peschiera into the great pentagonal stronghold whose walls — now UNESCO-listed — still ring its old town. For two centuries this has been northern Europe's favourite warm-water holiday, which is why a German family on bikes and a Veronese nonna buying peaches share the same gelato queue. The south is flat, sunny and easy; the north, an hour away, turns to sheer cliffs and windsurfers.

Where to base yourself

Six towns, one clear winner

Without a car, where you sleep decides what your week feels like. Two southern towns sit right on the fast Milan–Venice railway; the rest you reach by ferry or bus. Here's the whole south shore, weighed for a family with a nine-year-old — and the one we'd book.

★ Our base
Peschiera del Garda
Direct train · Gardaland shuttle · boat hub
A canal-laced Venetian fortress town and the only place that's both on the fast railway and a flat five-minute walk from platform to lakefront. The free Gardaland shuttle, the boat-rental docks, beaches and a ferry pier are all here. Car-free heaven.
Direct train
Desenzano del Garda
Direct train · lively · ferries
The lake's "real city" — the busiest, most year-round town, with a buzzy harbour, an old castle, the most restaurants and nightlife, and the main fast-rail stop. The strong runner-up base, and the ferry hub for Sirmione.
Bus / ferry
Sirmione
No station · the showpiece
A pedestrian town on a thread-thin peninsula, ringed by water, entered over a drawbridge into a moated castle, with Roman ruins and thermal baths at its tip. The most beautiful — and most crowded. Magic to visit; a touch of a faff (and pricey) as a base.
Bus / bike
Lazise
Walled · near the parks
The closest walled lake town to Peschiera — a little harbour, medieval gates, a calm gelato-and-stroll lakefront, and the flat bike path linking the two. Quiet-pretty, and minutes from Gardaland and the water parks.
Bus / ferry
Bardolino
Lakefront · wine town
A lively lakefront resort and the name behind the light, easy-drinking red. A long promenade, plenty of family hotels with pools, and the separated cycle path running right through it.
Ferry · scenic
Garda & Punta San Vigilio
Cheerful · the prettiest cove
A cheerful lakefront town and, just past it, the "Portofino of Garda" headland — a cypress-lined point with the sheltered, playground-backed Baia delle Sirene swimming cove. The pretty end of the east shore.

Getting around · no car required

Trains, ferries & buses

The whole south lake is a connected, car-free web — a fast railway across the bottom, public ferries to every town, buses along both shores, flat bike paths from the door, and a free park shuttle. Here's how the pieces fit.

Rail
The Milan–Venice line

Peschiera & Desenzano sit on the fast line. From Peschiera: Verona ~13–20 min, Milan ~57 min, Venice direct. Station to old town is a five-minute flat walk.

Ferries
Navigarda

The government boat operator links Peschiera, Desenzano, Sirmione, Lazise, Bardolino, Garda & the north lake. The 2026 summer timetable runs May 16 – Oct 4 — you're fully covered.

Buses
ATV & Arriva

ATV routes 483 & 164 run the east shore hourly in high season; Arriva LN026 links Desenzano–Sirmione–Peschiera–Verona. In peak season the bus stops at Colombare with a shuttle into Sirmione's old town.

Theme park
The Gardaland shuttle

A free shuttle runs from Peschiera station to Gardaland (~2 km) every ~30 min. No car, no taxi, no parking — just hop on.

Two wheels
Flat bike paths

Separated, traffic-free paths run Peschiera–Lazise–Bardolino along the shore, and out the back along the Mincio to the watermill village of Borghetto (~17 km). Rentals deliver to your hotel.

On the water
Your own boat

The most fun "transport" of all: a self-drive motorboat from Peschiera's port — Sirmione in ~15 minutes, beaches with no road to them, swims with no one else around. See the boat day ↓

The one catch · getting to the airport on Jul 10

There's no direct train from the lake to Milan Malpensa — the fastest route changes once at Milano Centrale onto the Malpensa Express, about 2 h 10 m door-to-airport. For the 4 PM flight, take a last lake swim, then leave Peschiera around 10 AM and you'll reach MXP with a comfortable buffer. (A verified research pass refuted the "cheaper-but-slower" routings floating around online — the via-Milano-Centrale path is the right one.)

A suggested rhythm

Five days on the lake

Big-ticket mornings, pool-and-gelato afternoons, lakefront evenings — built around the two fixed facts of the week: Sirmione's castle is shut on Monday Jul 6, and the boat wants a calm morning before the midday wind. Everything here is flexible; the weather and the nine-year-old get a vote.

  • Sun · Jul 5 · arrive
    In from Monterosso, settle into Peschiera
    The ~5 h ride via Milano Centrale (book the direct Intercity to Milan, then ~1h20 on). Drop the bags, walk the star-fort walls and canal bridges, first gelato in the old town, dinner by the water. No agenda.
  • Mon · Jul 6 · the big one
    Gardaland (with a SEA LIFE heat-break)
    Free shuttle from the station; Italy's #1 theme park is open till 23:00 in July. Ride at opening, escape the afternoon heat in the air-conditioned SEA LIFE aquarium, then ride again at dusk with the lights coming on. (Sirmione's castle is closed today — perfect day to skip it.)
  • Tue · Jul 7 · the magic one
    Captain your own boat to Sirmione
    Rent a motorboat in Peschiera (no licence needed under 40 hp), launch in the calm morning, and motor across to the fairy-tale peninsula. Anchor off Jamaica Beach for a swim straight off the bow, then walk into the castle and the Roman Grotte di Catullo. Home before the afternoon wind builds.
  • Wed · Jul 8 · a day in the city
    Verona day-trip (15 minutes by train)
    A direct ~15-min hop east: the Roman Arena older than the Colosseum is famous, Juliet's balcony and the love-note wall, gelato on Piazza delle Erbe, the fortified Ponte Scaligero over the river. Back to the lake by evening for a swim and supper — you keep your hotel and your pool.
  • Thu · Jul 9 · the lazy / wild card
    A pure lake day — your pick
    Earn the down-day: beach-and-pool at Peschiera or Lazise, Caneva Aquapark for the hottest afternoon, a ferry town-hop to Bardolino and the Baia delle Sirene cove, or the one big northern adventure — Malcesine and the rotating Monte Baldo cable car. Borghetto's watermills by bike if legs are willing.
  • Fri · Jul 10 · home
    Last swim, then the run to Malpensa
    One final lake morning, then leave Peschiera ~10 AM: train to Milano Centrale, change to the Malpensa Express, and into the 4 PM nonstop for Newark. Door-to-airport about 2¼ hours with a buffer.
The best of the lake

What to see & do

A theme-park capital, a castle in the water, a rotating cable car, Roman ruins you swim beneath, and a safari park up the road — the south lake is improbably loaded for a nine-year-old, and good for the grown-ups too.

The headliner

Gardaland · + SEA LIFE & the water park

Italy's #1 theme park, essentially next door — big coasters, a brilliant kids' zone, lots of shade and theming, and a nine-year-old clears most of the family rides. The complex also holds the indoor, air-conditioned SEA LIFE aquarium (the perfect midday heat-break) and the Legoland Water Park.

Do itFree shuttle from Peschiera station. July hours 10:00–23:00 — rest the afternoon, ride at dusk. Buy timed tickets online (cheaper); the Park + SEA LIFE combo beats two separates.
The highlight

Captain your own boat

The very best version of a Sirmione day: rent a small motorboat right in Peschiera, captain it across yourselves, and arrive past the moated castle. Anchor off Jamaica Beach — a flat limestone shelf below the Roman ruins where the water runs turquoise and shallow — and swim straight off the boat.

No licenceUnder 40 hp needs no licence — and Dad has a real boating licence besides. Launch in the morning; the thermal "Ora" wind chops up the south basin after noon. Full boat brief ↓
The showpiece town

Sirmione · castle, Romans & thermal baths

A 13th-century castle rising straight out of the lake — drawbridge, ramparts to climb, water on every side (~€8, free under 18). Beyond it the Grotte di Catullo spread a vast Roman villa ruin through an olive grove. And the Aquaria thermal spa lets the nine-year-old in on a family "Junior" session (from ~€28).

Plan itCastle & Grotte are closed Mondays (Jul 6) — that's why the boat day is Tuesday. Go early; the pedestrian town is mobbed by midday in July.
The northern wow

Malcesine & the rotating cable car

From the castle-topped town of Malcesine, the Funivia's upper cabins rotate 360° as they climb to 1,760 m on Monte Baldo — the whole lake and the Alps wheeling below, cool air, easy trails, paragliders launching off the top. The north's genuine kid "wow."

The catchMalcesine is far north — a long ferry or a bus up the eastern shore. Worth one full day if you want the mountains; book the cable car online (~€27 adult / ~€17 junior).
Down-time

Beaches, bikes & water parks

The glue between the big days: Peschiera's Cappuccini / Pioppi beaches (playground, pedal-boats, calm water) and Lazise's shallow shore; the flat, traffic-free Mincio cycle path to the storybook watermill village of Borghetto; and Caneva Aquapark near Lazise for the hottest afternoon.

TipMost beaches are pebble — pack water shoes. The lake has no tides and no real waves; mornings are glass.
Ferry-hopping

The eastern-shore towns

Ferry- or bike-hop the calm walled towns of the east shore: Lazise's harbour and gates, Bardolino's wine-and-promenade buzz, and Garda town with the sheltered Baia delle Sirene cove at Punta San Vigilio — an olive-grove park with a playground, shade and protected water.

Ferry dealThe Navigarda "Happy Family" fare lets 2 adults bring up to 2 kids (4–11) free — a town-hopping boat day for about the price of two tickets.
Hidden gems & rainy-day cards
  • Parco Natura Viva (Bussolengo) — a drive-through-style safari (lions, giraffes, rhinos) plus a walking zoo, ~20 min inland; the shadier, cooler pivot for a hot or cloudy day.
  • The Peschiera ramparts at dusk — walk the star-fort walls and canal bridges when the day-trippers thin and the swifts come out; a gelato from La Romana (try the "Crema 1948") seals it.
  • Borghetto sul Mincio — the end of the easy bike path: a tiny medieval village of working watermills straddling the river, with riverside lunch terraces. Pure storybook.
  • Movieland & Caneva Aquapark (Canevaworld) — a movie-themed park and a big water park near Lazise; the obvious second theme-park day if Gardaland leaves them wanting more.
The day the trip is built around

Captain your own boat

Dad holds a boating licence and knows his way around a powerboat — so this isn't a chartered tour with a skipper. It's your own little hull, your own throttle, and a morning where the family becomes its own crew on the biggest lake in Italy.

How it works

Under Italian rules a rental boat up to 40 hp (30 kW) needs no licence at all — just ID and a deposit. That's plenty of power to potter the south basin, and it neatly sidesteps any question about an American licence being accepted. Two operators sit right at Peschiera's central port, beside the Navigarda pier:

  • Rent Booking (Lepanto Marine) — self-drive open motorboats, half- or full-day.
  • Peschiera Boat Rent — self-drive "open" boats, same waterfront.

Reckon roughly €100 half-day / €160 full-day on a 40 hp boat (confirm 2026 rates, fuel and deposit when you book). Ask for a child life jacket and confirm the size for a nine-year-old.

The plan of the morning
Timing
Go early

Garda's thermal "Ora" wind builds from the south around midday and chops up the open south basin. Launch soon after the rental opens for glassy water; be back before it freshens.

The run
Peschiera → Sirmione

About 15 minutes across to the peninsula. Idle in past the castle, then anchor off Jamaica Beach — turquoise shallows below the Roman ruins — and swim straight off the bow.

Safety
Simple rules

Keep ~300 m off the shoreline, slow right down near swimmers and beaches, life jackets on the small crew, and watch the wind line on the water to your north.

⛵
For the small first mate

On Lake Garda you can rent a boat and drive it yourself with no licence at all, as long as the engine is under 40 horsepower — and your dad has a real boating licence and knows how to drive a powerboat. So one morning your family can become its own little crew: cast off from a 16th-century fortress, steer your own boat across an Italian lake, drop anchor below a castle that's been standing in the water for 700 years, and jump straight off the bow into the clear, shallow water. Not many nine-year-olds can say their dad captained them to a castle.

Practical · high summer

July, handled

Heat
Front-load the day

July tops ~32 °C. Hit Sirmione and Gardaland at opening, keep an air-conditioned/animal day (SEA LIFE, Natura Viva) in your back pocket, and let the pool carry the afternoons.

Crowds
Book the big-ticket bits

It's peak season. Reserve the hotel now, buy Gardaland tickets online to skip the line, book the boat ahead, and pre-book the Aquaria session if you want it.

Beaches
Pack water shoes

Most shoreline is pebble, not sand. The lake has no tides and no real waves — calm and warm by July, and glassy in the mornings.

The water day
Wind before noon

Whatever you do on the lake, the calm is in the morning; the "Ora" wind comes up after midday. Plan boats and long swims early.

Tickets
Ferries & passes

For a town-hopping day, the Navigarda lower-lake day pass plus the "Happy Family" fare (kids free) is the deal. Buses take cash or contactless on board.

Airport
Leave by ~10 AM Jul 10

No direct train to Malpensa — one change at Milano Centrale, ~2¼ h door-to-airport. A relaxed morning still makes the 4 PM flight comfortably.

A moment to seek

Mid-morning on a flat, glassy lake. You've cut the engine a hundred metres off the Sirmione headland; the castle and the old Roman columns shimmer on the point, and the water under the boat is the impossible green of a swimming pool. Your daughter is perched on the bow in her life jacket, working up the nerve — and then she's in: a shriek, a splash, hair slicked back, grinning up at you from water so clear you can count the pebbles four metres down. You are the captain of a small boat on the biggest lake in Italy, anchored below a 700-year-old castle, and absolutely nobody is in a hurry.
