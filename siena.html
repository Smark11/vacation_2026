Know before you go

Tuscany, honestly

Siena is the great medieval city of the Tuscan hills — a perfectly preserved warren of brick streets falling away from the shell-shaped Piazza del Campo, ringed by 17 fiercely proud neighbourhoods. Around it spreads the Val d'Orcia: cypress-lined roads, lone farmhouses, and a string of wine-soaked hill towns. It's romance over theme-park thrills — which suits a kid who doesn't like rides — but it asks something the lake never did: to really do the hill towns, you'll want a car or a tour.

📅 When & how long

You're here in peak July — hot, golden, busy. Four nights is right: two for Siena itself, one or two for the hill towns. Base inside Siena's walls (walkable, car-free) and day-trip out.

🚆 Getting there

No high-speed rail to Siena — it's ~4½ h by regional train from Monterosso via Pisa or Florence, then escalators up from the station to the hill town. Full route ↓

🚗 The car question

Siena, Monteriggioni & San Gimignano work by bus. The Val d'Orcia (Pienza, Montalcino, Montepulciano) really wants a rental car or a day-tour. The honest take ↓

🎟️ A no-rides kid

Plenty to love without a single coaster: climbing towers, a citywide contrada "treasure hunt," pecorino tastings, thermal springs, world-champion gelato and a fortress to run.

☀️ July heat

Hilltops are exposed — hats, water, and an early start; melt the afternoons with a gelato, a cool church, or an agriturismo pool. The towns empty out beautifully in the early evening.

✈️ Getting home

Siena → Milan is ~5 h, so you'll travel west on Jul 9 and overnight near Milan/Malpensa for the Jul 10 flight. Details ↓

Getting there & back · car-free

From Monterosso to the hill

Siena has no fast train — it's a couple of regional hops down through Pisa or Florence, then a ride up from the valley station into the old town. Plan on about four and a half hours, two changes, and roughly €30–40 each.

The cleaner option is usually via Pisa Centrale then the Siena regional (via Empoli); going via Florence S.M.N. works too, and from Florence a direct Autolinee Toscane coach to Siena (~1¼ h) is sometimes faster and comfier than the train. The decision that shapes everything

Car, bus, or tour?

This is the one thing to settle first. Siena and a couple of towns are easy car-free; the postcard Val d'Orcia is not — even the official Tuscany tourism board says the ideal way to see these towns is by car, with tours and buses as the workable alternatives. Here's the honest breakdown, and what we'd actually do.

Easy car-free
Siena · Monteriggioni · San Gimignano

Siena is all on foot. Monteriggioni is a ~35-min bus; San Gimignano is ~1–1¼ h by bus (change at Poggibonsi). These three you can do on public transport without stress.

Hard car-free
The Val d'Orcia

Pienza, Montalcino, Montepulciano & the thermal springs are each ~1¼–1½ h by infrequent bus, sparse on Sundays — and chaining two in a day is impractical with a kid. This is where a car or tour earns its keep.

Best of both
Rent a car for a day, or take a tour

Either rent a small car for 1–2 days (agencies by Siena's station; ~€45–70/day) — every town has a ZTL, so park in the lots outside the walls — or take a guided Val d'Orcia day-tour from Siena (driver, ~€90–130 pp), the easy car-free way to bag several towns.

What we'd do

Base car-free in Siena — then one Val d'Orcia day with wheels

Sleep inside Siena's walls (walkable, no car needed), give the city two unhurried days, and bus out to Monteriggioni and San Gimignano. For the Val d'Orcia magic — Pienza, Montalcino, a thermal soak — either book a day-tour or rent a car for one day. If a pool and farm life appeal more than city nights, flip it: spend your last 1–2 nights at a Val d'Orcia agriturismo (with a car) and let the hill towns be your back yard.

The city · ~2 days

Siena, up close

A city that stopped in the Middle Ages and never quite restarted — and all the better for it. No rides required: towers to climb, a striped marble cathedral, a hospital full of secrets, and a citywide animal treasure-hunt.

The heart

Piazza del Campo & the Torre del Mangia

The great shell-shaped square where the whole city tilts inward — sit on the warm brick and watch it. Then climb the Torre del Mangia, ~300 steps up one of Italy's tallest medieval towers for a view across the rooftops to the hills.

Plan itTower entry is timed and capped — go early. The Palio bareback horse race is run right here on Jul 2 & Aug 16 (you just miss it, but the contrade fever lingers).
The showstopper

The Duomo & the marble floor

A zebra-striped cathedral of black-and-white marble, with an inlaid mosaic floor of 56 panels that — by lucky timing — is uncovered exactly during your visit (27 Jun–31 Jul 2026); it's hidden under protective boards most of the year. Add the fresco-blazing Piccolomini Library and the "Gate of Heaven" rooftop walk along the upper galleries.

Book itAn OPA SI Pass bundles the cathedral, library, baptistery & museum. Pre-book the timed Gate of Heaven rooftop and your entry — July is hot and busy.
For the kids

The contrada treasure-hunt

Siena is split into 17 contrade — neighbourhoods, each with an animal emblem: the snail, the panther, the dragon, the she-wolf, the caterpillar. Spot the flags, fountains and street plaques as you wander; give the nine-year-old a list and a mission. It turns the whole maze into a game.

AlsoRun the ramparts of the Fortezza Medicea at sunset (there's an enoteca of Tuscan wine inside for the grown-ups).
Underground

Santa Maria della Scala

One of Europe's oldest hospitals, now a vast museum you can get lost in — frescoed pilgrim halls, an Etruscan collection in the cellars, and shadowy underground passages. Cool, uncrowded, and quietly thrilling for a curious kid. Cap the day with gelato on the Campo.

BonusA welcome air-conditioned escape from the afternoon heat.
A day or two out

The hill towns

The reason people fall for Tuscany. Each is a walled, walkable jewel — and each tags how easy it is to reach without a car. Green = bus-friendly from Siena; orange = wants a car or a tour.

Bus ~35 min

🏰Monteriggioni

A tiny, perfect ring of walls on a hilltop — the "crown" Dante saw from afar. You can walk the whole circle in a few minutes, climb sections of the rampart-walk, and count the 14 towers. The easiest hill town to reach, and a quick, complete kid-win.

  • Walk the medieval walls; two gates, one little square, total fairy-tale scale.
  • A great half-day or a stop paired with another town.
Reach~35-min bus from Siena — easy car-free
Kid appeal★★★☆ — small, climbable, unmistakably a castle
Bus ~1–1¼ h

🗼San Gimignano

The "medieval Manhattan" — fourteen stone tower-houses still spike the skyline, the survivors of seventy that rival families once built ever-higher. Climb the tallest, then settle a serious gelato debate on the main square.

  • Climb the Torre Grossa (the one tower you're allowed up) for the tower-forest view.
  • Gelateria Dondoli on Piazza della Cisterna — a multiple world-champion gelato.
  • A small, gleefully grim torture museum for older/braver kids.
ReachAutolinee Toscane Line 130, ~1–1¼ h (often a change at Poggibonsi) — doable car-free
Kid appeal★★★★ — towers + the world's best gelato
Car / tour

🧀Pienza

A pocket-sized Renaissance "ideal city" built to order by a 15th-century pope — a few perfect streets (one named Via dell'Amore, the next Via del Bacio, "kiss street") opening onto a terrace with the Val d'Orcia laid out below. And it's the home of pecorino di Pienza sheep's cheese.

  • The panoramic walls — the classic Val d'Orcia view, cypresses and all.
  • A pecorino tasting in a tiny cheese shop (a fun, low-stakes kid taste-test: fresh vs aged vs truffle).
ReachLine 112 (Buonconvento–San Quirico–Pienza); sparse — roughly every ~4 h, Mon–Sat, little Sunday service — far better by car or tour
Kid appeal★★★☆ — tiny, pretty, cheese + a great view
Car / tour

🍷Montalcino

A proud hill town wrapped around a 14th-century fortress you can climb — ramparts to run, sweeping views, and (for the grown-ups) an enoteca of Brunello, one of Italy's greatest reds, inside the castle walls.

  • Climb and walk the fortezza ramparts — a real castle, low crowds.
  • Brunello tasting for the parents; sweeping Val d'Orcia panoramas for everyone.
ReachOn a separate, infrequent line via Buonconvento — realistically a car or tour
Kid appeal★★★☆ — a climbable fortress + big views
Car / tour

🛢️Montepulciano

A dramatic ridge-top town of Renaissance palaces and the noble red Vino Nobile. Beneath the streets, ancient cantine (wine cellars) burrow into the rock — atmospheric to tour, cool on a hot day — and the Piazza Grande has stood in for Hollywood (Twilight, Gladiator II).

  • Walk down into a cantina carved in the tufa rock — caves, barrels, cool air.
  • The cinematic Piazza Grande and long ridge-top views.
ReachEnd of Line 112 (sometimes a change at Buonconvento), ~1¼–1½ h, sparse — much better by car/tour
Kid appeal★★★☆ — underground cellars + film-set squares
Car / tour

♨️Bagno Vignoni & the hot springs

A village whose central "piazza" is a giant steaming Renaissance thermal pool — gorgeous, though you can only look (it's a protected monument). To actually soak, families head to the free Fosso Bianco at Bagni San Filippo nearby — white travertine cascades and warm woodland pools you can wade right into. A genuinely magical, no-rides kid memory.

  • Wade in the warm natural pools of Fosso Bianco (free; water shoes help).
  • Gawp at Bagno Vignoni's steaming stone "square" — there's no other piazza like it.
ReachCar or tour only (very limited bus)
Kid appeal★★★★ — bathing in a natural hot spring
Also out there, if you have the wheels
  • Val d'Orcia drives — the cypress-lined roads and lone-farmhouse hills around San Quirico and the "Gladiator" landscapes; the whole valley is a UNESCO site. Best with a car and no schedule.
  • Volterra & Cortona — two more striking hill towns (Etruscan walls; Under the Tuscan Sun) — both reachable but a longer haul; one's plenty for a 4-day trip.
  • An agriturismo afternoon — many farms do family lunches, animals and a pasta-making hour; the easiest "kid + countryside" win if you're based out of town.
Together · hands-on with a 9-year-old

Things to do as a crew

Siena's magic is partly just being there — but here are the hands-on, do-it-together things that turn a medieval city into a kid's adventure, no rides required.

🍝

Make pici together · a cooking class

The Tuscan classic to do as a family: a hands-on pici class, rolling the fat local pasta by hand (no machine — all thumbs and palms, which kids love), often with a pumpkin tortello and tiramisù too, then eating the lot. Siena schools like Fonte Giusta Academy and home-cook hosts via Cesarine run short, family-welcoming sessions.

Book it · ~2–3 h · from ~€40–70 pp · kids welcome · reserve ahead
🗼

Climb the Torre del Mangia

~300 steps up one of Italy's tallest medieval towers — a real "we did it" for a 9-year-old, with the whole brick city and the hills unrolling below at the top. Timed and capped, so go early.

Plan · book a morning slot; not for the very claustrophobic
🐌

The contrada treasure-hunt

Arm the kid with the list of 17 contrada animals — snail, dragon, panther, she-wolf, caterpillar — and hunt the flags, fountains and street-plaques across the city. The best free game in Tuscany, and it teaches the whole map.

Free · all week, everywhere you walk
🧀

A pecorino taste-test

In Pienza (or a Siena cheese shop), line up fresh vs. aged vs. truffle pecorino and let the 9-year-old be the judge — a low-stakes, giggly way into Italy's food culture, with honey and pears alongside.

Pair · with the Val d'Orcia day
♨️

Wade in a wild hot spring

At Bagni San Filippo, white travertine cascades and warm woodland pools you can actually bathe in — a free, slightly secret, properly magical soak in the forest. (Car or tour to reach it; bring water shoes.)

Where · Fosso Bianco, Val d'Orcia · free
🏰

Run a castle wall

Let off steam on real ramparts — the Fortezza Medicea in Siena (free, with an evening enoteca for the grown-ups), the tiny circuit at Monteriggioni, or Montalcino's fortress on the Val d'Orcia day.

Best at · the Fortezza at sunset
A suggested rhythm

Your four days

Base in Siena, give the city its due, and reach for the Val d'Orcia once with a car or a tour. Two cooler-morning sights, gelato-and-shade afternoons, golden evenings on the Campo.

  • Sun · Jul 5 · arrive
    In from Monterosso (~4½ h), settle into Siena
    Train via Pisa/Florence, escalators up to the old town. Drop the bags, find your contrada, an evening passeggiata and the first gelato on Piazza del Campo.
  • Mon · Jul 6 · the city
    Siena, unhurried
    Torre del Mangia at opening, then the Duomo + the marble floor + the Piccolomini Library + the Gate of Heaven rooftop (pre-book the timed slots — and you're in luck: the floor is uncovered 27 Jun–31 Jul). Lunch, Santa Maria della Scala in the heat, the contrada treasure-hunt, sunset at the Fortezza.
  • Tue · Jul 7 · the Val d'Orcia
    Pienza · Montalcino · a thermal soak (car or tour)
    The big one: rent a car for the day or take a guided Val d'Orcia tour — Pienza's terrace + pecorino, Montalcino's fortress, and a warm wade at Bagni San Filippo. Cypress roads the whole way.
  • Wed · Jul 8 · towers by bus
    San Gimignano + Monteriggioni
    Car-free day: bus (Line 130) to San Gimignano for the towers and Dondoli's world-champion gelato, with a stop at the tiny walled circle of Monteriggioni on the way back. Check the timetable the night before — buses are sparse. Or swap in Montepulciano's cellars if you kept the car.
  • Thu · Jul 9 · westward
    A last Siena morning, then on to Milan
    One more turn around the Campo, then the ~4½–5 h journey west (Florence → Milano Centrale → Malpensa Express) to your final-night base near the airport, ready for the Jul 10 flight.
🐌
For the small detective

Siena is secretly divided into 17 neighbourhoods called contrade, and they take it very seriously — each has its own flag, fountain, song, colours and an animal: the Snail, the Panther, the Dragon, the Caterpillar, the She-Wolf, the Tortoise. Twice a year they race horses bareback around the Campo in the Palio, and grown adults cry when they win. As you walk, look up: the flags and little fountains tell you which contrada you're standing in. See how many of the seventeen animals you can find — it's the best free game in Tuscany.

À table · Tuscan country cooking

Pici, pecorino & Brunello

Hearty, simple, peasant-rooted and superb — bread, beans, pork, sheep's cheese, fat hand-rolled pasta, and some of the planet's great red wine. Kids eat well here.

Pici
Fat, hand-rolled spaghetti — the Siena pasta. Try it all'aglione (garlic-tomato), cacio e pepe, or with a wild-boar ragù. A guaranteed kid-pleaser.
Pecorino di Pienza
Sheep's-milk cheese from the Val d'Orcia — fresh and mild to aged and sharp, sometimes in walnut leaves or rubbed with ash. A fun taste-test for the kid.
Bistecca alla fiorentina
The towering, rare-grilled T-bone steak — a proper Tuscan feast for the table to share, usually sold by weight.
Ribollita & pappa al pomodoro
Peasant comfort food: ribollita (twice-boiled bread-and-bean soup) and pappa al pomodoro (bread-and-tomato) — cosy, mild and cheap.
Cantucci & the great reds
Almond cantucci dipped in sweet Vin Santo to finish — alongside three legends within an hour: Brunello, Vino Nobile and Chianti.
Gelato
Tuscany takes gelato seriously — Siena has world-class gelaterie. The daily ritual after a tower climb, eaten on the warm slope of the Campo.

A moment to seek

Late afternoon, the heat finally breaking. You've climbed down off the cathedral roof and the Campo has filled with that low gold light that makes the whole city look lit from inside the bricks. Your daughter is lying back on the warm slope of the square with a gelato — fragola, naturally — counting contrada flags out loud and getting them wrong, and a swift is screaming somewhere up around the tower you climbed this morning. Nobody is going anywhere. Tomorrow there are cypress roads and a hot spring in the woods, but right now there is just brick and shadow and strawberry, and seven hundred years of someone else's history holding perfectly still around your kid.
